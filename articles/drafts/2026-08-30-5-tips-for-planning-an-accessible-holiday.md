## Start with the holiday you actually want

When you think about your next getaway, what is the first thing that comes to mind? Maybe it’s the sound of waves on a Mediterranean beach, the bustle of a historic city, or the simple joy of a relaxing cruise. 

It is easy for the logistical requirements of mobility to take center stage before you’ve even picked a destination. It can feel like you have to "filter out" options until only a few remain. However, the best way to begin is by focusing on the experience itself. 

Don’t start with a list of constraints; start with your goals. Do you want to spend your days exploring local markets? Are you looking for a peaceful retreat where you can relax with family? By identifying the type of experience you want first, the planning process becomes about finding the right way to get there, rather than just trying to find a "suitable" room.

When you work with the team at Limitless, they help keep that vision front and center. The goal isn't to make your holiday an "accessibility exercise"—it’s to ensure that the practical details of your journey support the holiday you’ve been dreaming of.

## Define what "accessible" means for you

One of the most frustrating parts of planning online is the word "accessible" itself. For one person, it might mean a ground-floor room; for another, it might mean a specific door width, a certain height for a grab rail, or a lack of steps between the lobby and the beach. 

Because "accessible" isn't a one-size-fits-all term, it is helpful to pinpoint exactly what matters to your independence. Instead of hoping that a generic "accessible" label covers your needs, identify your non-negotiables.

Does the layout of the bathroom need to be a certain way for your mobility scooter? Is it essential that there are no steps between your accommodation and the nearest dining area? By identifying these specific needs early, you can cut through the guesswork. When you speak with the Limitless team, they can take these specific details and advocate for you, ensuring the accommodation actually works for your daily routine.

## Think about the whole journey, not just the room

It’s easy to get caught up in the details of the hotel or apartment. While the room is your sanctuary, a holiday is made up of many moving parts. 

Think about the journey as a whole. How will you get from the airport to the hotel? Is the local transport in the area accessible? If you’re planning to visit several landmarks, are those sites easy to navigate? 

Consider the logistics of your equipment as well. If you use a mobility scooter or a wheelchair, how will it be handled during transfers? By looking at the "big picture" of your trip, you can identify potential hurdles before they happen. This holistic approach helps ensure that the transition between your home and your holiday destination feels as smooth as possible.

## Ask the questions that matter before you decide

You shouldn't have to spend hours scouring dozens of websites to compare accessibility features. Sometimes, a simple "yes" or "no" from a specialist can save hours of research. 

Instead of trying to interpret a generic description of a room, ask direct questions. You might ask about the height of a counter, the specific width of a doorway, or the availability of a lift that serves both the lobby and the dining room. 

Getting these answers early reduces uncertainty. It allows you to make a confident decision about whether a destination is right for you before you commit. This proactive approach means that when you finally arrive at your destination, you aren't wondering if the layout will work—you already know it will.

## Speak to someone who understands the details

Sometimes, the most effective way to plan is to simply have a conversation. 

It can be exhausting to try and interpret what a photo or a bulleted list means for your specific needs. A human conversation allows you to explain your situation in detail, ensuring that nothing is lost in translation. You can talk through your concerns about transport, your specific requirements for a bedroom, and the overall flow of your trip.
