# Article

The appeal of Tenerife lies in its diversity. It isn't just a beach destination; it is a land of dramatic volcanic landscapes, varied coastal environments, and a well-established tourism infrastructure that caters to a wide range of needs. For those seeking a wheelchair accessible holiday in Tenerife, the island offers a wealth of possibilities, from the gentle sands of the south to the more rugged, scenic north.

However, when mobility is a primary consideration, the "broad" appeal of a destination can feel overwhelming. A beach might be beautiful, but is it accessible? A hotel might be near the coast, but is the path from the lobby to the pool paved? These are the practical questions that turn a general desire for a holiday into a manageable plan.

### Navigating the journey to the island

The first step of any holiday is the journey itself. Tenerife is a major international destination, and the infrastructure to support passengers with reduced mobility is well-established.

**Airport Assistance**
Tenerife North (TFN) and Tenerife South (TFS) airports are accustomed to providing assistance for passengers with mobility needs. This typically includes help through the terminal, to the aircraft, and occasionally assistance with luggage. When planning, it is helpful to understand exactly what "assistance" means for your specific situation—whether that is a wheelchairer needing a lift to the gate or a passenger using a mobility scooter who needs to ensure their equipment is accommodated on the aircraft.

**Local Transport and Mobility**
Once on the island, how you move around will dictate your experience. Many resorts are designed with large, flat areas, making them easy to navigate with a wheelchair or mobility scooter. 

In the local towns, public buses are common, but their suitability varies. While many are accessible, space can be limited during peak hours. For many guests, a private taxi or a dedicated transport service offers more certainty. If you plan to explore the island's coastline or visit local markets, a mobility scooter can provide a significant amount of independence. The key is knowing where the terrain remains flat and where the cobblestones of older village streets might present a challenge.

*If you would like to discuss how transport options in Tenerife might suit your specific mobility needs, speak with a travel advisor about your plans.*

### Choosing the right base: Accommodation

Finding a wheelchair friendly holiday in Tenerife involves looking deeper than a simple "accessible room" label. In a coastal environment, the difference between a room that is "accessible" and one that is "convenient" can be significant.

**The Importance of the Bathroom**
One of the most frequent concerns for guests is the bathroom. A room may have a walk-in shower, but is there enough space for a wheelchair to turn around? Is the grab rail in the right position? When looking at hotels in Tenerife, it is worth considering the proximity of the room to the main facilities. You may want to be close to the pool or the restaurant without having to navigate long corridors or ramps.

**Ground Floor vs. Accessible**
In many Spanish resorts, a "ground floor" room does not always mean "accessible." Some may still require navigating a small step or a narrow door. When exploring options, it is better to ask specific questions about the layout of the poolside areas and the ease of access to the dining rooms.

### Exploring the island

Tenerife offers a variety of experiences that can be enjoyed regardless of mobility levels. 

**The Coastline**
Many of the main beaches in the south are served by well-maintained promenades. These are ideal for staying close to the water while remaining on a firm, level surface. Some beaches also offer specific sections designed for those with physical disabilities, often featuring nearby ramps and accessible facilities.

**Excursions and Nature**
While the interior of the island is more mountainous, several excursions are popular and accessible. Boat trips from the coast are a common way to see the coastline and local marine life, with many boats designed to accommodate those with mobility issues. Furthermore, several parks and botanical gardens offer paved paths, providing a way to experience the island’s unique flora without needing to traverse rugged terrain.

The goal of a holiday in Tenerife should be to experience the sun, the sea, and the scenery. By identifying the specific elements that matter most to you—whether that is a peaceful spot by the pool, a trip out on the water, or a stroll along the promenade—you can narrow down the best area of the island to base yourself in.
