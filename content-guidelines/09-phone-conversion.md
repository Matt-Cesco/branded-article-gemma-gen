# Phone Lead CRO Guide

The primary objective is to generate qualified phone enquiries, not simply telephone-link clicks.

A good phone CTA helps the reader understand why a conversation is valuable: their requirements are individual, details may need checking and a specialist can help them compare suitable options.

## When To Introduce The Phone CTA

Introduce a phone CTA when:

- The article has answered the main question.
- The reader may need personalised advice.
- Accessibility details vary by traveller, property, transport or date.
- The article has built enough trust.
- A phone conversation would genuinely help clarify suitability.

## When Not To Introduce A Strong Phone CTA

Do not introduce a strong phone CTA:

- Before answering an informational query.
- When the article has not established relevance.
- As a substitute for useful content.
- When the reader likely needs only a simple factual answer.
- When the CTA would pressure rather than help.

## Strong CTA Formula

Use:

Concern + reassurance + value of human conversation + action.

Example:

> "Every traveller's requirements are different. If you're unsure whether this type of holiday will work with your wheelchair, mobility scooter or other accessibility requirements, speak with our travel team. We can talk through what you need and help you understand the available options."

CTA button:

> "Talk to our travel team"

## CTA Categories

### Soft CTA

For informational intent. Use after answering the query.

### Contextual CTA

For accessibility questions. Use when a specific practical detail may need checking.

### Destination CTA

For destination research. Use after destination suitability has been discussed.

### Holiday CTA

For high-intent users. Use once relevant holiday options or categories have been introduced.

### Complex-Needs CTA

For people who need personalised advice. Use where room layout, equipment, care or transport are central.

## Avoid

Avoid:

- "Buy now."
- "Hurry."
- "Act now."
- "Don't miss out."
- "Book before it's too late."
- Generic "Contact us."
- Generic "Learn more."

Use these only if there is a genuine, user-relevant reason, and never to create false pressure.
