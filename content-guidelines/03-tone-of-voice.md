# Tone Of Voice

The primary tone is clear, empathetic, warm and reassuring.

Also write in a way that feels confident, calm, human, respectful, conversational and knowledgeable.

Use British English.

## Sentence Length

Prefer short-to-medium sentences. Long sentences are acceptable only when they remain easy to follow.

Good:

> "If you use a mobility scooter, check how it will be stored, transported and charged during your trip."

Avoid:

> "Prospective customers utilising mobility equipment should ascertain logistical feasibility in relation to storage, transport and charging requirements prior to committing to an itinerary."

## Paragraph Length

Paragraphs should normally be 1-4 sentences. Use shorter paragraphs for complex accessibility information, important cautions and CTAs.

## Vocabulary

Prefer everyday English. Use specialist terms only when they are useful, and explain them when needed.

Prefer:

- "step-free access"
- "wet room"
- "transfer"
- "mobility scooter"
- "walking aid"
- "care support"

Avoid vague phrases:

- "accessibility provision"
- "inclusive facilities"
- "mobility solutions"
- "differently abled travel experiences"

## Active Voice

Use active voice whenever natural.

Prefer:

> "Our team can talk through the details with you."

Avoid:

> "The details can be discussed by our team."

## Direct Reader Language

Prefer "you" rather than impersonal phrasing.

Prefer:

> "You may want to ask whether the transfer vehicle can carry your wheelchair."

Avoid:

> "Travellers may wish to enquire as to transfer suitability."

## Empathy

Acknowledge concerns without dramatizing them.

Prefer:

> "It is understandable to want the bathroom layout confirmed before you choose a hotel."

Avoid:

> "Bathroom uncertainty can ruin a holiday."

## Reassurance

Never use empty reassurance.

Bad:

> "Don't worry, everything will be fine."

Good:

> "If you're unsure whether the bathroom layout will work for you, our team can talk through the available facilities before you decide."

## Positivity

Focus on possibilities without pretending planning is unnecessary.

Bad:

> "Wheelchair users face many difficulties when travelling."

Better:

> "Travelling with a wheelchair may require some additional planning, but the right accommodation and transport can make a significant difference."

## Clarity

Bad:

> "Our tailored accessibility provision enables individuals with diverse mobility requirements to facilitate their travel experience."

Good:

> "Tell us what you need and our team can help you explore suitable options."

## Preferred Writing Examples

1. "Accessibility can vary between hotels, rooms and destinations, so it is worth checking the details that matter most to you."
2. "If you use a wheelchair, ask about room layout, bathroom access and transfer arrangements before you book."
3. "A mobility scooter can make a holiday easier, but transport and storage need to be checked in advance."
4. "The right destination depends on your mobility, travel style and the level of support you want."
5. "Our team can help you compare options and understand what may suit your requirements."
6. "If you are travelling with a carer or partner, check how the room and itinerary will work for both of you."
7. "Some excursions may involve uneven ground, steps or longer walking distances."
8. "Ask whether step-free routes are available in the areas you expect to use most."
9. "A phone call can be useful when you need specific details checked."
10. "This guide explains what to consider before choosing an accessible cruise."
11. "You do not need to know every technical term before you call."
12. "Tell us what matters to you and we can help identify the right questions."
13. "For many travellers, confidence comes from understanding the practical details early."
14. "Accessible travel should still feel enjoyable, relaxing and personal."
15. "If a wet room or hoist is essential, make sure availability is confirmed for your dates."
16. "A destination may work well for one wheelchair user and less well for another."
17. "This is why individual requirements should be discussed before booking."
18. "Airport assistance can help with parts of the journey, but arrangements should be requested in advance."
19. "If you are comparing destinations, think about transport, accommodation and what you want to do each day."
20. "When you speak with us, we can help you understand which options are worth exploring."

## Examples To Avoid

1. "Our holidays are perfect for every disabled traveller."
2. "Don't let disability hold you back."
3. "Wheelchair-bound guests will love this resort."
4. "Book now before accessible rooms disappear."
5. "This destination is 100% accessible."
6. "Special needs travellers are catered for."
7. "Sufferers can finally enjoy a holiday."
8. "We guarantee a stress-free trip."
9. "All transport is accessible."
10. "Elderly people need simple holidays."
11. "The disabled deserve sunshine too."
12. "Our accessibility solutions facilitate inclusive mobility."
13. "This miraculous holiday will change your life."
14. "Simply call us and everything is sorted."
15. "Accessible travel is impossible without specialists."
16. "Click here for more."
17. "Learn more."
18. "Hurry, don't miss out."
19. "Everyone can join every excursion."
20. "No planning is required."

## Before And After Rewrites

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "This hotel is fully accessible." | "This hotel offers a range of accessibility features. Speak with our team to check whether the room layout and facilities match your requirements." |
| "Disabled travellers should call us now." | "If you would like to talk through your accessibility requirements, our team can help you understand the available options." |
| "Don't worry about airport assistance." | "Airport assistance should usually be requested in advance. Check what support is available for your journey before you travel." |
| "We cater for special needs." | "We can discuss accessibility, mobility, care or support requirements before you choose a holiday." |
| "This cruise is ideal for wheelchair users." | "This cruise may suit some wheelchair users, depending on cabin layout, ship access and excursion choices." |

## Headline Rules

- Make the topic clear.
- Match search intent.
- Avoid clickbait.
- Use accessible-travel terms naturally.
- Do not promise more certainty than the article can support.

Good:

> "Can You Take a Mobility Scooter on a Plane?"

Avoid:

> "The Ultimate Secret to Effortless Disabled Travel"

## Introduction Rules

- Answer the search query quickly.
- Avoid generic holiday scene-setting.
- Mention accessibility relevance early when appropriate.
- Reassure with practical information, not empty comfort.
- Do not open with a hard sell.

## CTA Tone Rules

CTAs should feel like helpful invitations.

Use:

- "Talk to our travel team."
- "Ask us to check the details."
- "Discuss your accessibility requirements."
- "Explore suitable accessible holidays."

Avoid:

- "Buy now."
- "Act fast."
- "Don't miss out."
- "Contact us" as a standalone CTA.

## Explicit Prohibitions

Do not use:

- Infantilising language.
- Pity.
- Inspiration clichés.
- Exaggerated positivity.
- Excessive exclamation marks.
- "Special needs" unless context absolutely requires it.
- Corporate jargon.
- Unnecessarily medical language.
