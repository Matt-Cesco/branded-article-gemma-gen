# Search Intent Framework

Classify intent before planning the article. CTA timing, structure and internal links should follow the reader's mindset.

## INFORMATIONAL

Example: "Can you take a mobility scooter on a plane?"

Reader mindset: They want a direct answer and practical guidance.

User question: "What is allowed, what should I check and what happens next?"

Article format: Explainer, checklist, practical guide or answer-led article.

CTA timing: Late or after a genuinely useful section.

CTA strength: Soft.

Ideal internal links: Related guides, accessibility information, relevant travel planning pages.

Trust requirement: Moderate. Accuracy matters more than persuasion.

Recommended structure: Answer first, explain rules or considerations, list what to check, add accessibility-specific advice, finish with a soft phone CTA.

## PROBLEM / QUESTION

Example: "How do wheelchair users get on a coach?"

Reader mindset: They may be worried about a specific practical barrier.

User question: "How does this work in real life, and what support might be available?"

Article format: Step-by-step guide, process explainer or practical FAQ.

CTA timing: Contextual, after the process has been explained.

CTA strength: Soft to moderate.

Ideal internal links: Transport guidance, relevant holiday types, enquiry path.

Trust requirement: High if accessibility, safety or support is involved.

Recommended structure: Explain the process, identify variations, state what must be checked, address individual requirements, introduce Limitless support.

## DESTINATION RESEARCH

Example: "Is Tenerife wheelchair friendly?"

Reader mindset: They are considering a destination but need accessibility context.

User question: "Could this destination work for someone with my requirements?"

Article format: Destination guide with accessibility considerations.

CTA timing: Mid-to-late, after discussing the destination honestly.

CTA strength: Moderate.

Ideal internal links: Destination page, related holidays, destination-specific accessibility content, phone enquiry path.

Trust requirement: High. Destination claims can easily become overbroad.

Recommended structure: Give balanced destination overview, discuss access considerations, highlight what varies, connect to relevant Limitless options, invite a call.

## COMMERCIAL RESEARCH

Example: "Best wheelchair accessible holidays in Europe"

Reader mindset: They are comparing options and may be open to providers.

User question: "Which types of holiday should I consider, and how do I choose?"

Article format: Comparison guide, curated options article or decision guide.

CTA timing: After useful comparison criteria and suitable examples.

CTA strength: Moderate.

Ideal internal links: Holiday categories, destination pages, relevant holiday pages, phone CTA.

Trust requirement: High. The article must justify recommendations.

Recommended structure: Define "best" by reader need, compare holiday types or destinations, explain accessibility checks, introduce Limitless expertise, include contextual CTAs.

## HIGH COMMERCIAL INTENT

Example: "Wheelchair accessible holidays Tenerife"

Reader mindset: They are close to exploring or enquiring about a specific type of holiday.

User question: "What options are available, and can they work for me?"

Article format: Holiday-focused guide, destination holiday page support article or commercial explainer.

CTA timing: Earlier than informational content, but still after relevance and basic usefulness.

CTA strength: Stronger, but never aggressive.

Ideal internal links: Relevant holiday pages, destination pages, accessible holiday category, phone enquiry path.

Trust requirement: Very high. Suitability claims must be careful and verified.

Recommended structure: Confirm relevance, explain available options, discuss accessibility suitability, add trust and reassurance, provide clear phone CTA.

## Intent Decision Logic

Ask:

1. Is the query asking for a factual answer? Use informational.
2. Is the query asking how a practical accessibility issue works? Use problem/question.
3. Is the query evaluating a place? Use destination research.
4. Is the query comparing options? Use commercial research.
5. Does the query include a specific holiday type, destination and accessibility modifier? Use high commercial intent.

When in doubt, prioritise the lower-pressure intent and earn the conversion through usefulness.
