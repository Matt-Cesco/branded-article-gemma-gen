# Topic Strategy

Automated keyword research is not being built yet, but every future topic should be judged through a consistent editorial and commercial lens.

Use this topic evaluation structure:

```yaml
topic:
primary_keyword:
search_intent:
search_demand:
limitless_relevance:
accessibility_relevance:
lead_potential:
commercial_relevance:
existing_content:
content_gap:
likely_internal_links:
likely_phone_conversion:
priority:
```

## Core Principle

High search volume alone does not make a good Limitless topic.

Prefer topics at the intersection of:

Search demand + accessible travel relevance + user need + Limitless expertise + lead potential.

## Priority Levels

### HIGH PRIORITY

Use when:

- The topic has meaningful search demand.
- The query relates directly to accessible travel.
- Limitless can credibly help.
- The reader may need personalised suitability advice.
- There is a clear phone enquiry path.
- Existing content does not already satisfy the intent.

### MEDIUM PRIORITY

Use when:

- The topic is useful and relevant.
- Lead potential is moderate.
- It supports a wider topic cluster.
- It may assist readers earlier in the funnel.

### LOW PRIORITY

Use when:

- The topic is only loosely connected to Limitless.
- Lead potential is weak.
- The article would mainly be generic travel advice.
- Internal linking opportunities are limited.

### DO NOT CREATE

Use when:

- The topic has no meaningful accessible travel angle.
- The query cannot be answered accurately.
- The article would require unsupported accessibility claims.
- The topic duplicates existing intent.
- The content would attract irrelevant traffic.

### UPDATE EXISTING ARTICLE

Use when:

- An existing article already targets the same intent.
- The new angle is a subsection, FAQ or refresh opportunity.
- Facts have changed.
- Accessibility guidance can be improved.
- The current article underperforms but has the right URL and intent.

## Content Cannibalisation

Cannibalisation occurs when multiple articles compete for essentially the same query and reader need. It weakens clarity for both users and search engines.

Avoid separate articles when:

- The primary keyword is a close variation.
- The search intent is identical.
- The same answer would satisfy both queries.
- Internal links would point to competing pages.

Create separate articles only when the reader need is meaningfully different.

## Topic Decision Questions

Ask:

- Would a Limitless customer search this?
- Does the topic help someone assess accessible travel suitability?
- Can the article lead naturally to a phone enquiry?
- Can the article be accurate without inventing details?
- Is this better as a new article or an update?
- What internal page should the article support?
