# Trust And Reassurance

Trust should generally come before the primary conversion request. For accessible travel, readers need to feel that their concerns have been understood before they are asked to enquire.

## Trust Framework

Use this structure:

Concern -> acknowledgement -> practical information -> evidence -> reassurance -> next step.

Example:

> "If you need a particular bathroom layout, it is sensible to check before booking. Room features can vary, even within the same hotel. Where verified information is available, include it clearly. If more detail is needed, invite the reader to speak with the team so the right questions can be asked."

## Reviews

Reviews are useful when they are specific. Specific reviews are more valuable than generic praise.

Useful review topics include:

- Accessible rooms.
- Accessible transport.
- Care support.
- Staff support.
- Booking experience.
- Mobility equipment.
- Feeling listened to.
- Individual requirements being accommodated.

Never fabricate reviews, review counts, star ratings or customer quotations.

## Testimonials

Use testimonials only when supplied and approved. Do not paraphrase a testimonial into a stronger claim than the source supports.

## Advisor Expertise

When discussing Limitless expertise, focus on what the team can do:

- Talk through requirements.
- Explain holiday options.
- Help identify details to check.
- Discuss transport, accommodation and support considerations.
- Help readers understand whether an option is worth exploring.

Do not invent credentials, awards, training or processes.

## Verified Accommodation Information

State accommodation details only when verified. Include the scope of the information where relevant.

Good:

> "The room information provided for this holiday includes a wet room."

Avoid:

> "All rooms are accessible."

## Transport Information

Transport details may vary by destination, provider, route, vehicle and date. Do not imply transport suitability unless verified.

## Accessibility Processes

If Limitless has a verified process for checking requirements, describe it accurately. If no process detail is supplied, use cautious wording:

> "Our team can talk through your requirements and help check the details that matter to you."

## What Happens After Calling

Explain the value of the call:

- The reader can describe their requirements.
- The team can ask clarifying questions.
- Relevant options can be discussed.
- Important details can be identified for checking.
- The reader can decide whether to continue.

## Setting Expectations

Be honest:

- Not every holiday will suit every traveller.
- Some details may need checking.
- Availability can vary.
- Accessibility features can differ by room, ship, hotel or destination.
- A phone call is not a guarantee of suitability.

## Uncertainty Handling

When unsure, say what can be checked. Do not fill gaps with confidence.

Safe reassurance:

> "If this feature is essential, speak with our team before booking so the current information can be checked."
