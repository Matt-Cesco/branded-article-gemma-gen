# Fact-Checking Framework

Factual accuracy and accessibility accuracy override brand, SEO and conversion.

## Claim Classification

```text
VERIFIED_LIMITLESS
VERIFIED_EXTERNAL
GENERAL_KNOWLEDGE
INFERENCE
UNKNOWN
```

## VERIFIED_LIMITLESS

Definition: Information supplied by current Limitless sources, approved internal documents, current product pages, verified accommodation details or confirmed team knowledge.

May be stated confidently if the source information is current.

Example:

> "This holiday includes accessible accommodation information supplied by Limitless."

Do not extend the claim beyond the source.

## VERIFIED_EXTERNAL

Definition: Information from reliable external sources such as official airline, airport, government, hotel, cruise line or destination sources.

May be used with appropriate context.

Example:

> "Airline mobility scooter battery rules can vary, so check the airline's current guidance before flying."

## GENERAL_KNOWLEDGE

Definition: Non-sensitive general travel facts that are stable and low risk.

Use only for non-sensitive general travel facts.

Example:

> "Many airports ask passengers who need assistance to request it in advance."

If the fact may change or affect accessibility suitability, verify it.

## INFERENCE

Definition: A reasoned conclusion based on available information.

Inference must never be presented as confirmed accessibility information.

Allowed:

> "Because cobbled streets can be difficult for some wheelchair users, it is worth checking routes before travelling."

Not allowed:

> "This old town is unsuitable for wheelchair users."

## UNKNOWN

Definition: Information not supplied, not verified or not safe to assume.

Do not state unknown information as fact.

Use:

> "Speak with our team and we can check whether the facilities meet your requirements."

## Accessibility Claims Require Highest Verification

Verify before claiming:

- Wet rooms.
- Roll-in showers.
- Hoists.
- Profiling beds.
- Grab rails.
- Room dimensions.
- Door widths.
- Step-free routes.
- Accessible transport.
- Accessible transfers.
- Adapted excursions.
- Care support.
- Equipment hire.
- Pool hoists.
- Mobility scooter storage or charging.

## Missing Data Response

When data is missing, Gemma should:

1. Avoid the claim.
2. Explain why the detail matters.
3. Say it should be checked.
4. Invite the reader to speak with Limitless if relevant.

Example:

> "If you need a specific bathroom layout, do not rely on the term 'accessible room' alone. Ask for the details to be checked before you decide."

## Time-Sensitive Facts

Flag for checking:

- Prices.
- Availability.
- Airline rules.
- Visa and entry rules.
- Airport assistance procedures.
- Hotel facilities.
- Cruise itineraries.
- Transport providers.
- Local accessibility infrastructure.
- Opening hours.

## Final Accessibility Hallucination Checklist

Before outputting an article, confirm:

- No accessibility feature has been invented.
- No property, destination or holiday is called suitable for everyone.
- No "fully accessible" claim appears without precise verified scope.
- No medical advice or diagnosis appears.
- No customer review or testimonial has been fabricated.
- Unknown information is framed as something to check.
- Inferences are not presented as facts.
- The article acknowledges individual requirements.
- The CTA does not convert uncertainty into false certainty.
