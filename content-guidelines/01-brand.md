# Brand Guide

## Brand Purpose

Limitless Travel helps people enjoy travel with greater confidence by understanding and accommodating individual accessibility requirements wherever possible.

The brand should make travel feel more possible without pretending every option works for every person.

## Brand Personality

Limitless should sound:

- Warm.
- Human.
- Positive.
- Knowledgeable.
- Experienced.
- Calm.
- Helpful.
- Inclusive.
- Reassuring.
- Flexible.
- Practical.

## Desired Customer Feeling

The emotional progression should often be:

> "I wonder if this is possible."

to:

> "This sounds more possible than I thought."

to:

> "They seem to understand my concerns."

to:

> "I'd feel comfortable asking them about my situation."

## Brand Positioning

Limitless should not sound like:

- A clinical care provider.
- A generic mass-market booking engine.
- A hard-sales travel company.
- A disability information directory.

Limitless should sound like experienced accessible-travel specialists helping people find enjoyable holidays that work around individual requirements.

## Brand Language Rules

Use language that is practical and human. Make the reader feel understood, not categorised.

Prefer:

- "Talk through your requirements."
- "Explore suitable options."
- "Check the details before you decide."
- "Accessibility requirements."
- "Care or support requirements."
- "Travelling with a wheelchair."
- "Travelling with reduced mobility."
- "Step-free access where available and verified."
- "May be suitable depending on your requirements."
- "Our team can help you understand the options."

Avoid:

- "Perfect for everyone."
- "Fully accessible" unless thoroughly verified.
- "Confined to a wheelchair."
- "Suffers from."
- "Special needs" unless quoting a source or search phrase with care.
- "The disabled."
- "Elderly travellers" as a blanket label.
- "Hassle-free" when meaningful planning may still be needed.
- "Just book now."
- "Dream holiday guaranteed."

## Positive And Negative Examples

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "If you use a wheelchair, the right room layout and transfer arrangements can make a big difference." | "Wheelchair-bound travellers need special rooms." |
| "Our team can talk through your mobility requirements before you choose." | "Our holidays are suitable for all disabled people." |
| "This guide explains what to check before travelling with a mobility scooter." | "Everything you need to know about disabled travel." |
| "Accessibility can vary by hotel, room type and destination." | "The destination is accessible." |
| "You may want to ask about bathroom layout, step-free routes and transfer arrangements." | "The hotel has everything disabled travellers need." |
| "For some travellers, airport assistance can make the journey feel more manageable." | "Don't worry, the airport will take care of you." |
| "Tell us what you need and we can help you explore suitable holidays." | "Book your accessible holiday today before places go." |
| "A phone conversation can help us understand the details that matter to you." | "Call now to secure your dream trip." |
| "Some excursions may be more suitable than others depending on access, distance and transport." | "All excursions are accessible." |
| "If a hoist, wet room or specific bed setup is essential, ask us to check availability." | "Special equipment is available." |

## Brand Do

- Be specific about what can be checked, discussed or arranged.
- Make the next step feel useful.
- Show empathy through practical understanding.
- Keep the holiday experience present: relaxation, discovery, comfort, companionship and enjoyment still matter.

## Brand Do Not

- Over-medicalise travel content.
- Turn every paragraph into a sales pitch.
- Treat accessibility as a binary label.
- Use inspiration clichés.
- Make the reader feel like a problem to be managed.
