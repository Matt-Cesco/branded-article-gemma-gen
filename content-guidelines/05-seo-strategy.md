# SEO Strategy

SEO must focus on satisfying intent, not keyword density. The article should be as long as necessary to answer the topic well and no longer than useful.

## Primary Keyword

Choose one primary keyword that represents the central intent. The article should clearly satisfy that query. Use the primary keyword naturally in the SEO title, H1 where appropriate, introduction, metadata and at least one relevant heading if it reads naturally.

There is no keyword-density target.

## Secondary Keywords

Secondary keywords should support the primary topic. They may include synonyms, related questions and commercial modifiers.

Use secondary keywords only where they help the reader.

## Semantic Topics

Include the practical subtopics a reader would expect:

- Accessibility requirements.
- Accommodation.
- Transport.
- Airport or journey logistics.
- Equipment.
- Excursions.
- Care or support.
- Suitability questions.
- Relevant Limitless next steps.

## Search Intent

Before writing, classify intent using `06-search-intent.md`. Do not force a high-conversion structure onto a purely informational query.

## Topical Completeness

An article is complete when it answers the main query and the important follow-up questions a reader would naturally have.

Do not add filler to hit arbitrary word counts.

## Information Gain

Each article should add something useful beyond generic search results. For Limitless, information gain often comes from:

- Accessibility-specific considerations.
- Questions to ask before booking.
- Suitability caveats.
- Practical planning advice.
- When to speak with a specialist.

## User Questions

Identify 5-10 likely user questions before drafting. Answer the most important ones in the body and use 3-7 genuinely helpful FAQs.

## Internal Linking

Plan internal links before writing. Link to pages that help the reader continue their decision journey, such as destination pages, holiday category pages, relevant holiday pages, related articles and enquiry paths.

## Headings

Headings should be descriptive, specific and useful for scanning. Do not use playful or vague headings when the reader needs practical clarity.

Good:

> "What to check before taking a mobility scooter abroad"

Weak:

> "Things to know"

## Metadata

SEO titles should be clear and intent-matched. Meta descriptions should summarise the practical value and, where relevant, mention accessibility considerations.

Do not overpromise:

Bad:

> "The complete guide to fully accessible Tenerife holidays."

Better:

> "Find out what to consider when planning a wheelchair accessible holiday in Tenerife, including accommodation, transport and questions to ask before booking."

## URL Slug

Use short, readable lowercase slugs with hyphens:

- `/blog/wheelchair-accessible-holidays-tenerife`
- `/blog/can-you-take-mobility-scooter-on-plane`

Avoid dates unless required.

## Image Alt Text

Alt text should describe the image accurately and concisely. Do not stuff keywords.

Good:

> "Wheelchair user beside an accessible transfer vehicle at a hotel entrance."

Bad:

> "Best wheelchair accessible holidays disabled holidays accessible travel UK."

## Featured Snippet Opportunities

For question-led articles, provide a concise answer near the start. Use bullets or short paragraphs when the answer involves steps, checks or definitions.

## FAQ Opportunities

Use FAQs for real follow-up questions, not keyword variations. Each answer should be useful on its own and should not invent details.

## Cannibalisation Prevention

Do not create multiple articles targeting essentially identical intent. If two topics answer the same primary question for the same audience, combine them or choose one canonical article.

## Existing-Content Comparison

Before drafting a new article, compare it with existing content where available. If an existing article already targets the same intent, update that article instead.

## Search Intent Mismatch

If the keyword suggests a different intent from the proposed article, revise the angle.

Example:

- Keyword: "can you take a mobility scooter on a plane"
- Wrong angle: "Book accessible holidays with Limitless"
- Right angle: "Answer airline and battery rules first, then explain when specialist travel advice may help."

## Explicit SEO Rules

- No keyword-density target.
- No repeated exact-match keyword purely for ranking.
- No filler content to hit arbitrary word counts.
- No creation of multiple articles targeting essentially identical intent.
- No SEO claim may override factual or accessibility accuracy.
